# Forum Authoring Style V0 · 老乡不是一种人

Status: **content / world-writing guideline**  
Last updated: 2026-09-04

## 1. Core rule

The Traveler Forum must not sound like one maintainer wearing thousands of aliases.

Different travelers have different personalities, education, eras, confidence, trauma, humor, ambitions, moral limits, writing habits, and amounts of time left to write.

Forum voice diversity is a first-class content requirement, not decorative flavor.

## 2. Allowed emotional range

The forum should contain, among many others:

- cheerful / absurd / joking;
- frightened / panicked;
- confused / helpless;
- quietly practical;
- ambitious / ready to reshape the world;
- arrogant / overconfident;
- exhausted / cynical;
- tender / protective;
- lonely;
- grieving;
- furious;
- scholarly / methodical;
- terse / barely literate;
- lyrical / reflective;
- petty / grudge-holding;
- triumphant;
- resigned.

Do not balance these mechanically. A believable forum is uneven.

## 3. Writing-form diversity

Posts and replies may differ in:

- length: one sentence to very long serial reports;
- punctuation and formatting habits;
- vocabulary and education level;
- use of jokes, slang, memes, quotations, ledgers, checklists, verse, diagrams, or plain prose;
- willingness to explain context;
- confidence in conclusions;
- attitude toward later travelers;
- whether the author edits or returns to update the post.

A long technical post and a three-word panic reply should both feel native to the same forum.

## 4. Post life history

A forum topic may have a life beyond its first publication.

Possible states include:

- actively updated for years;
- edited after the author changes their mind;
- contradicted by later replies;
- abandoned without explanation;
- interrupted mid-sentence;
- later annotated as author missing / deceased / returned home / unknown;
- revived hundreds of years later because a new traveler encountered the same problem;
- quoted into later module releases;
- partially deprecated while some sections remain useful.

The author's later fate is lore context, not automatic proof that their advice was right or wrong.

## 5. Example: interrupted historical thread

A valid forum experience may look like:

```text
《穿成亡国之君的第一年：我做对了什么、做错了什么》
作者：大虞·永宁帝
发布时间：约三百年前

第一，不要急着改革。你连棋盘上有几颗棋子都数不清楚的时候，任何落子都是送死。
第二，找到一个你信得过的人。不是忠臣，是你愿意把后背交给他的人。
第三，如果你有能力保护的人不多，那就只保护最重要的那几个。其余的……

——正文在此中断——

回复 427：作者已于永宁三年殉国。
```

The emotional force comes from forum structure and missing continuation, not from a narrator explaining how sad the reader should feel.

## 6. Replies must also have voices

Replies should not become a uniform fact-checking chorus.

Different replies may:

- comfort;
- mock;
- argue;
- correct details;
- tell a related failure story;
- ask an embarrassingly basic question;
- recognize the author personally;
- report that the same advice failed in another world/region;
- add a maintainer annotation years later.

The forum should preserve disagreement when disagreement is real.

## 7. Author profiles

Authored seed content may optionally carry a lightweight author-writing profile, such as:

```text
voice temperament
education / technical fluency
preferred post length
humor tendency
certainty tendency
format habits
known relationships to other forum authors
```

This profile exists to preserve consistency across multiple posts by the same fictional traveler. It must not become a rigid personality generator.

## 8. Do not confuse voice with reliability

A funny post may be correct.
A polished scholarly post may be catastrophically wrong.
A terrified one-line warning may save lives.
A dead author's advice does not become sacred merely because the ending is tragic.

Reliability, review status, provenance, and applicability remain separate data dimensions.

## 9. Real community voices

Real community contributions should retain the contributor's actual writing voice where consent and moderation allow.

Maintainers may edit for privacy, formatting, safety, or clarity, but should not rewrite every contribution into house style.

If a real contribution is adapted into lore, preserve the contributor's chosen attribution / lore alias and mark it as real-community provenance.

## 10. Seed-content acceptance rule

Before a larger founding forum corpus is considered healthy, a random screen of topics should not read as if every author:

- knows the same amount;
- speaks with the same sentence rhythm;
- shares the same politics or ethics;
- has the same sense of humor;
- writes the same length;
- survives long enough to write a tidy conclusion.

A living forum needs unfinished lives as well as finished guides.
